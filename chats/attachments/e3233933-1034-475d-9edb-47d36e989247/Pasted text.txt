2. Overall mission workflow

Implement this workflow:

A. Configure the city, traffic, pedestrians, UGV route and deployment stops.
B. The UGV follows its dedicated lane to each deployment bay.
C. Assigned UAVs launch and survey that site's visible surroundings.
D. Actual camera detections populate a live catalogue of observed people
   and vehicles.
E. UAVs recover according to the existing carousel sequence.
F. After all configured sites have been surveyed, present the target catalogue.
G. I select one particular detected person or vehicle.
H. An available UAV launches, reacquires that target if necessary, and follows it.
I. Tracking ends when I stop it, the duration expires, the target is lost beyond
   the configured search period, or the aircraft must return.
J. The UAV returns, detects its assigned AprilTag, lands, disarms and docks.

Allow an optional mode in which I select a target before the full survey ends.
Keep “survey all sites, then select” as the default.

The catalogue must contain objects actually observed by cameras. Do not populate
it with every actor that exists in the simulated city.

4. UGV clearance and collision prevention

Calculate clearance from actual model dimensions, including:
- Chassis and wheels.
- Loaded aircraft.
- Tray supports.
- Maximum carousel movement envelope.
- Swept footprint during turns.

Deployment bays need additional clearance for carousel indexing and UAV approach
and departure.

Keep pedestrians, parked vehicles and moving traffic outside both the protected
travel corridor and active deployment areas.

Use physical collision geometry and measured body movement. Do not solve
collisions by disabling collisions, making obstacles transparent to physics,
or moving an obstructing actor away after contact.

Retain the existing stalled-carrier protection. If actual body progress stops,
hold the mission and explain the cause.

Normal scenarios must demonstrate zero UGV contacts with pedestrians or vehicles.
Deliberate fault scenarios must demonstrate a controlled stop.

5. Autonomous pedestrian behaviour

Give each pedestrian a route on the connected sidewalk network.

Include:
- Continuous walking with suitable walking animation.
- Different walking speeds, approximately 1.0–1.6 m/s initially.
- Pauses at corners, benches or building entrances.
- Changes of direction and route choice.
- Local spacing so pedestrians do not continually overlap.
- Some stationary people who later start walking.

Pedestrians must stay on sidewalks and designated public areas. They must not
walk through buildings, vehicles or the protected UGV corridor.

Introduce crossings only at designated locations on the normal traffic network.
Coordinate crossing access with traffic signals or intersection reservations.

Align walking animation, heading and foot placement with measured movement.
Avoid sliding feet, floating bodies and models embedded in the ground.

Standard Gazebo animated actors alone are insufficient evidence of physical
collision behaviour. If used for appearance, synchronize them with an appropriate
physical representation and validate visual/collision alignment.

Document whether pedestrians use controlled kinematic collision bodies or
dynamic bodies. Do not describe either as a full biomechanics simulation.

6. Autonomous vehicle behaviour

Give every moving vehicle a valid route through directed lanes.

Include:
- Cars, buses and trucks where supported by existing assets.
- Lane following.
- Smooth acceleration and braking.
- Suitable turning paths and turning radii.
- Following distance.
- Queuing behind slower or stopped vehicles.
- Traffic signals or conflict-free intersection reservations.
- Occasional stops and a mixture of parked and moving vehicles.

Vehicles must not cut across sidewalks, buildings, deployment bays or the UGV lane.

Use wheel-actuated physical vehicle models where practical. If some background
vehicles use controlled kinematic motion, state that explicitly and preserve
their physical occupancy and camera-visible movement.

Wheel rotation and steering must agree with actual motion.

Start with modest traffic speeds that the validated UAV tracking capability
can follow. Measure aircraft closing-speed margin before raising target speeds.

A faster vehicle may legitimately escape tracking. Do not teleport the UAV,
slow the target secretly, or use an unlimited aircraft speed to conceal this.

7. Simulation authority and synchronization

Send measured actor poses and animation states to the browser. Interpolate
between updates for smooth rendering, but do not create a separate browser world

Keep units, coordinate frames and timestamps explicit.

8. Camera-based detection and tracking

Retain the existing YOLOX detector initially. Add a tracking layer rather than
replacing the entire perception stack without evidence.

Support the existing detectable classes:
- Person.
- Car.
- Truck.
- Bus.

Evaluate a lightweight multi-object tracker such as ByteTrack as the baseline.
Account for camera motion using synchronized camera calibration, aircraft pose
estimates and suitable image-motion compensation.

Evaluate appearance-assisted association only where needed to reduce identity
switches or support reacquisition. Measure its processing cost before enabling
it across multiple aircraft.

Operational perception must use:
- Actual camera pixels.
- Detector outputs.
- Camera calibration.
- Timestamped aircraft pose estimates.
- Previous visual observations.

Do not feed simulator actor names, hidden actor IDs, exact target poses,
planned actor routes or future destinations into detection, identity association,
target selection or follow control.

Keep simulator ground truth in a separate evaluation path. It may drive the city
and measure accuracy, but must not make tracking appear more capable.

Maintain the existing 640×480, 10 Hz camera configuration as the initial baseline.
Increase resolution or inference demand only after measured performance tests.

9. Target identity and the discovery catalogue

Create a session-scoped target registry.

Each entry should contain:
- A perception-generated target ID, such as Person-007 or Vehicle-012.
- Detected class and class confidence.
- A recent camera crop or thumbnail.
- First-seen and last-seen timestamps.
- Time since the last observation.
- Observing UAV and survey site.
- Observation history.
- Estimated position, speed and heading when available.
- Position uncertainty.
- Identity confidence, separate from class confidence.
- Current state: visible, temporarily occluded, stale, lost or archived.

Require repeated observations before confirming a candidate.

Avoid duplicate entries for the same continuing observation. For observations
from different UAVs, associate candidates using time, estimated position and
visual evidence.

Do not assume two similar-looking people are the same individual. When evidence
is insufficient, retain separate candidates or mark the association uncertain.

A catalogue entry is an observed track, not a guarantee that the system can
identify that individual permanently across arbitrary camera gaps.

Keep historical detections available after surveying, but clearly mark stale
entries. Selecting a stale target must trigger reacquisition before tracking
is declared active.

12. Occlusion, target loss and reacquisition

When the selected target disappears:
- Preserve its last confirmed observation.
- Predict movement only for a bounded time.
- Increase uncertainty as observation age increases.
- Search a bounded area based on the last estimated position and velocity.
- Use observed appearance and movement continuity to assess candidates.

If several plausible candidates exist, show the ambiguity and request target
reconfirmation instead of silently following another person or vehicle.

Never switch to the nearest object of the same class merely to keep tracking
active.

Provide configurable:
- Tracking duration.
- Lost-target search duration.
- Reacquisition confidence.
- Follow altitude and distance.
- Return reserve.
- Behaviour when the target leaves the permitted operating area.

On failure to reacquire, report LOST and execute the configured loiter/return
policy.
